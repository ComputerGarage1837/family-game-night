First draft and second pass

  • Cleaner shapes. Each pixel is averaged from 9 samples, so edges and thin parts like the blade hold steady while animating.
  • Cast shadows. Parts shade what sits behind them: under the pauldron, the jaw, the shield and the belt.
  • Painterly colour. Every colour ramp drifts cooler in shadow and warmer in light, the way pixel artists pick colours.
  • Softer outlines. The outline takes the colour of what it wraps and lightens on lit edges. Stray single pixels are cleaned up.
  • More detail. Layered, gilded pauldron, knee guards, hip plates, boot straps, a wrapped grip and gem pommel, sun heraldry on the tabard and shield, and a face placed pixel by pixel.
  • Follow-through. The cape and hair trail behind the body when it moves.

At game size

The same model, rendered straight to a 96 px frame, the size it would fight at in a full unit of five.

How it's drawn

Because a frame is generated from a pose, a new animation is a handful of numbers, and a palette swap re-colours every frame at once.

The attack, frame by frame

Runs at 16 frames per second. Like OB64's melee units, Rowan runs up to the target, strikes and hops back into formation.
